# The 50-person boardroom

October 2026. Fifty agents, each playing a different kind of person (a 78-year-old widower who lives alone, a night-shift ER nurse,
a single dad of three, a pharmacist, a farmer, a hospice caregiver, a CPA, a wheelchair user, a person in recovery, a grumpy skeptic who
hates apps, and so on), were asked the same open question with no topic limits:

> If you had an app where you could monitor your day-to-day life and control everything from one place, what would you want to track,
> check in on, be reminded about, or have it do for you? What modules would make your day-to-day easier?

They answered 250 ideas. One more agent merged them into the list below. "Demand" is how many of the 50 personas asked for something
in that group. Priority is their average "how much would I use it" score (5 = every day).

### What the main ones do
- **Home Maintenance & Chore Rhythm:** repeating jobs reset from the day you *actually* did them (furnace filter, smoke-detector batteries,
  trash night), plus a separate Fix-It list by voice with photo and room. Shows only what is due. Chores can be assigned to a profile.
- **Bills, Subscriptions & Renewals:** due dates, amounts, paid check, free trials about to convert, insurance and registration renewals,
  a "due in the next 7 days" strip on Today. Manual entry only, no bank login.
- **Appointments, Questions & Call Log:** questions for each doctor captured by voice between visits and shown the morning of the visit,
  prep rules (fasting, what to bring), a one-sentence after-visit note, results-pending, and a call log with reference numbers.
- **Refill, Supply & Expiry Runway:** pills left, days of supply, refill dates, expiry on inhalers and first-aid items, a warning before you
  run out on a Friday night.
- **Emergency Info Card:** one offline large-print screen: allergies, conditions, current meds (pulled from Meds), doctors, insurance, contacts.
- **Energy Pacing & Good-Day/Bad-Day Planner:** a morning energy tap; heavy tasks hide on bad days with no guilt; a "how do you feel 24-48 hours
  later" check to catch boom-and-bust cycles. Uses the pain and activity data already collected.
- **Waiting-On:** open loops other people owe you (quote, refund, callback, repair) with a nudge date and a copyable follow-up text.
- **Quick Capture Inbox:** one voice button for any thought; sort it later into a to-do, calendar entry or journal note.

## Quick wins (small, high demand)
Home Maintenance & Chores · Bills & Renewals · Quick Capture Inbox · Appointments & Questions · Refill Runway · Emergency Info Card ·
Checklists & Routines · Waiting-On follow-ups.

## Bigger bets
- Safety check-in that automatically alerts someone if you miss it (needs a text/push server; start with a share-based version).
- Care log shared by several people (baby, parent with dementia, an aide): needs cross-device sync and accounts.
- Reliable alarms and push for chores, bills and meds: one push server would unlock all reminders.
- Weather-aware daily brief (garden, yard, freeze warnings): needs outside data.
- Cross-device sync so Kevin and his father can share a chore list while keeping medical data private.
- Receipt photos: camera, storage, export and privacy handling.

## How people want to use it
- One tap or one spoken sentence to log something. If it takes longer, people quit within a week.
- The Today screen should show only what is due or needs attention, not a 15-card dashboard.
- Repeating chores need "interval from last done", and a skip or snooze that never scolds.
- One calm morning summary and quiet hours; keep loud alerts for what truly matters (meds, bills due, safety).
- Voice and big buttons for dirty, gloved, one-handed or stiff-fingered use.
- Separate lists for different jobs (fix-it vs to-do vs chores) so items do not rot in one mixed list.
- Capture first, sort later: one inbox beats deciding which module something belongs to.
- Link modules (groceries to meals and supplies, chores to helpers, sleep and energy to pain and tasks) so patterns show up.
- Search-first notes with no folders.
- Anything shared (reports, handoff sheets, requests) is started by the user: PDF, copy or share sheet.

## Cautions (the panel was nearly unanimous)
- **No streaks, badges, points or "you missed 3 days" alerts.** About 45 of 50 said these would make them delete the app
  (recovery, PTSD, chronic illness and ADHD users especially).
- Too many notifications train people to mute everything, including medication alarms. Make extra nudges opt-in and group them.
- **Never store passwords, full account numbers, SSNs or document scans.** Store locations and names only ("will is in the blue toolbox").
- No bank linking or bank logins. Money entry stays manual (or a CSV import on the device).
- No medical advice: no interaction checks, dose suggestions, risk scores or symptom interpretation. Log and show only.
- Do not claim a food, label or canning time is safe from AI guesses.
- Do not auto-call 911 or claim fall or heart-event detection from phone sensors (false alarms, false confidence).
- No auto-texting or auto-posting, and no tracking another person's location without clear consent.
- Care, recovery and symptom data is very sensitive: keep it on the device, offer an optional lock, keep it off the lock screen.
- A relapse or missed day must never wipe history; allow a quiet restart.
- Avoid smart-home and device integrations (every brand differs) and long setup forms.
- Watch readings are never presented as medical-grade (already a project rule). Keep real personal data out of this repository.

## Surprising ideas worth a look
- **Worry Parking Lot:** park a worry with a revisit date and sort it each evening (a therapist's tool).
- **Waiting-On list:** what others owe you; most to-do apps have nothing like it.
- **Access Scout:** private notes on places (ramp, door width, bathroom, parking), from the wheelchair user.
- **Incident Capture button:** one tap starts a time-stamped record (photos, location, other party, voice account) and a symptom timeline
  that feeds both an insurance claim and the doctor report.
- **Money-saved-by-staying-sober counter** next to the day count.
- **Impulse-buy wait list** that asks "still want it?" in three days.
- **Home inventory as a PDF for insurers**, using the same engine as the doctor report.
- **Leaving-the-room checklist** that learns which item you forget most.
- **Boom-and-bust pattern view:** a big Tuesday followed by a flat Thursday.
- **Pet and livestock log** reusing the med-reminder and report engine for vets.

## Suggested build order
1. Home Maintenance & Chore Rhythm: highest demand, small, and chores were specifically asked for.
2. Bills, Subscriptions & Renewals: second-highest demand, an obvious Today-screen win.
3. Appointments, Questions & Call Log: strengthens the doctor report, fits post-injury recovery.
4. Refill, Supply & Expiry Runway: builds on Meds, prevents running out of a medication.
5. Emergency Info Card: cheap, assembled from data the app already has, high safety value.
6. Quick Capture Inbox: one voice button that feeds To-Do, Calendar and Journal.
7. Energy Pacing & Good-Day/Bad-Day Planner: ties pain, activity and tasks together for recovery.
8. Spending Quick-Log & Safe-to-Spend: high demand and simple.
9. Checklists & Routines: small, reusable, good for foggy days.
10. Meal Plan & Pantry: links to Groceries and Food Log.

## Examples of what individual personas picked as their number one
- 78-year-old widower living alone: an "I'm OK" daily check-in that alerts a contact if missed.
- Night-shift ER nurse: a sleep and shift planner so no reminder wakes her or lands while she works.
- Single dad of three: a kids hub (forms, rides, school events) combined with the calendar and groceries.
- College student with ADHD: a deadline tracker that picks a start date and shows only today's few tasks.
- Retired veteran with back pain: a good-day/bad-day planner that ties sleep, pain and mood to what he takes on.
- Pharmacist: refill and supply runway ("running out with no refills left is the number one failure I see").
- Wheelchair user: Access Scout, private accessibility notes on places.
- Widow managing finances alone: bills and due dates, so she never misses one or lets a policy lapse.
- Physical therapist: a home-exercise log with pain per exercise, because the gap between what she prescribes and what patients do is her biggest blind spot.
- Family caregiver for a parent with dementia: a shared, time-stamped care handoff note that feeds a doctor-ready summary.
